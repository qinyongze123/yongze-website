I needed a bracket for the [AIS project](机器人-自动注射系统.html). After designing it in SolidWorks, I printed it on the Bambu Lab X1 Carbon 3D printer in the lab at KI (Karolinska Institutet), using purple Bambu Lab PLA Galaxy filament. One end of the bracket mounts on a moving frame, and the other end has to support an ultrasound probe system weighing a few hundred grams. The structure itself is very simple: a small plane joined perpendicularly to a main plane, a few millimeters thick. What surprised me was how strong the printed part turned out to be. I had meant to print it as a prototype to test the dimensions and then have it made by metal CNC, but when I test-fitted it, it could already almost support the weight of the probe. So I made a second version, thickening the small plane as much as possible and adding ribs, and the whole structure became very stable.

Since I had built a 3D printer myself before, I was very interested in this machine's performance, so I studied it carefully while it was printing, and this machine does a lot of things very well. The filament is stored in a drying box, and before printing it first extrudes some material into a waste area, which empties old material left in the nozzle and also preheats it as a side effect; I assume color and filament changes are handled by this same step. As for auto-leveling, I looked it up and it is reportedly done with a pressure sensor. The slicer is Bambu Studio, with its own heated bed and filament management and well-tuned print parameters. The printing process was also enjoyable, with an internal camera monitoring its state in real time. The finished part comes off easily, with a flat surface and high strength.

![Printing on the X1C](求职相关/创客/拓竹x1c初体验，3D打印机械连接件/用x1c打印中.png)

I have completely bowed down to Bambu Lab, hands up in surrender; the feeling is no different from Li Hongzhang visiting New York (the Qing statesman who was awestruck by what he saw in America in 1896). My takeaway: an individual's power is small, and capital's power is enormous. I used to have a bad habit: whenever I saw any product, I'd have a geek's mindset, and my heart would automatically say: "This thing is simple, I could make this too, the cost is low, it's not worth the price." But society runs on division of labor, and everyone doing their own job is the optimal solution for efficiency. Thinking about it now, much of the time I should focus my time on one thing (whether work or a big project), and use the money it brings to buy what I need. I am gradually coming to understand people who spend a lot on a set of tools or an ergonomic chair. Perhaps they could build a comfortable chair themselves. But why not do what they're better at to earn money, and then use the money to buy a product made by someone else? I truly need to believe in the capitalist market economy.
